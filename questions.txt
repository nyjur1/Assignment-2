(1) After a program has made some calls to malloc and free,
heap memory can become fragmented, 
meaning that there are chunks of free heap space interspersed
 with chunks of allocated heap space (Dive Into Systems, Section 2.4). 
 The allocations have a failure rate of 38.5108% because, although there may be 
 enough free memory, the free memory is split into smaller blocks so there may not 
 be one continuous block large enough for certain requests. 

(2) In plot-throughput.png t2.out has a better throughput. We see as the bytes 
 become larger it combines the free blocks more efficiently and can more easily reuse the 
 memory. 

(3) In plot-fail_rate.png t2.out has a better fail rate. When allocation size 
 requests increase, there is a consistent and much lower number presented. The blocks
 that were freed are now handled appropriately.
